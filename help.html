1. What is the Curriculum Ontology?

An ontology is a formal way to categorize and map entities in a specific domain. Just like biologists map genes to specific functions, this system maps Educational structures (like Course and Module) directly to Sustainability concepts (like SDG 6 or Life-Cycle Assessment).

2. Outgoing vs. Incoming Relationships

The ontology relies on RDF-style Subject-Predicate-Object relationships. Subclasses inherit these relationships dynamically.

  • Outgoing Relationships: Properties where the current term acts as the Subject. E.g., If you view Assessment, its outgoing relationship is evaluates (pointing to Learning Outcomes). Since Quiz is a child of Assessment, it automatically inherits this outgoing property.
  • Incoming Relationships: Properties where the current term acts as the Object. E.g., If you view Activity, its incoming relationship is hasActivity (because a Learning Outcome "has" an Activity).

3. SDG Learning Objectives

The system maps UN-official objectives using three pedagogical domains:

  • Cognitive: Understanding the knowledge behind the SDG.
  • Socio-emotional: Advocating and building awareness.
  • Behavioural: Taking direct action or designing solutions.

4. Visual Graph View

The D3.js Knowledge Graph visualizes the literal RDF triplets of a specific course. Nodes are colored by their category (Blue for Curriculum, Green for Sustainability). You can drag nodes to explore how a specific Module branches out into both Activities and SDG Targets simultaneously.

5. The CAMPUS Framework

The recommendation engine structures course upgrades using the CAMPUS methodology:

  • Confirm SDGs
  • Align to content
  • Map to Credit
  • Perform assessments
  • Update strategies
  • Sustain improvements
